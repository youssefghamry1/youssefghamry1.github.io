1. Role of the Bill of Quantities (BOQ) in Procurement

A Bill of Quantities (BOQ) is the standardized financial pricing matrix issued within a tender package that decomposes project work, hardware units, software licenses, and professional labor into discrete, measurable line items. By forcing every competing vendor to price identical line descriptions and unit quantities, procurement committees can evaluate offers apples-to-apples without hidden bundling asymmetries.

In public procurement systems such as the Saudi Etimad portal, the BOQ forms an indispensable legal pillar: unit rates entered in the financial schedule lock the exact contractual pricing for any variations, additions, or quantity reductions ordered during project delivery.

2. Standard Columns & Tax Compliance in Pricing Schedules

BOQ Column Authoring & Governance Function Common Audit Trap to Prevent
Item Serial & Activity Code Unique tracking reference matching SOW deliverables and invoicing milestones. Altering serial numbers or deleting sub-rows invalidates automated portal imports.
Unit of Measure (UOM) Defines billing granularity (Each, License/Year, Man-Month, Lump Sum, Meter). Pricing a 36-month recurring license as a single 1-year unit cost.
Estimated Quantity Baseline numeric volume specified by the buyer for uniform comparison. Modifying buyer quantities manually instead of seeking portal clarification.
Unit Rate (Excl. VAT) Legally overriding cost per single unit entered in digits and written words. Leaving rows blank or marking "Included"—must enter explicit numeric pricing or "0" if permitted.
Extended Line Total & VAT Quantity × Unit Rate plus statutory VAT (e.g., 15% in KSA) rolled into Grand Total. Decimal rounding mismatches between Excel formulas and official PDF price letters.

3. Item-Rate BOQ vs. Lump-Sum Contract Pricing

Unit-Rate / Re-measurable BOQ

Final contractor payments follow actual verified site quantities multiplied by locked BOQ unit rates. Heavily used in civil construction, cabling, hardware rollouts, and variable SLA staffing where initial quantities remain estimates.

Fixed Lump-Sum Schedule

Contractor commits to deliver the complete defined SOW at a fixed total sum broken across delivery milestones (e.g., 20% Design, 40% UAT, 40% Go-Live). Unlisted incidental work needed to complete the scope is absorbed by the contractor.

4. How Committees Handle Arithmetic & Pricing Errors

Under the Saudi GTPL and most public procurement statutes, financial committees enforce strict mathematical hierarchy rules when auditing BOQs:

  • Words Prevail Over Figures: Where a discrepancy occurs between a written price in words ("One Hundred Thousand Riyals") and digits ("10,000"), the written wording legally prevails.
  • Unit Rate Prevails Over Line Total: If Quantity × Unit Rate does not match the bidder’s printed Line Total, committees recalculate the line and grand total using the Unit Rate.
  • Unpriced Mandatory Items Trap: Leaving mandatory items unpriced or blank can trigger automatic financial disqualification—or force the winning vendor to absorb that line item at zero revenue if law permits committee re-attribution.
  • Abnormally Low Pricing Review: Bids falling drastically below official cost estimates (often >25–35% below engineering estimates) face mandatory financial sustainability audits and rejection if unsubstantiated.

5. Pre-Submission Commercial QA & Envelope Isolation

Financial QA requires two non-negotiable verification sweeps 48 hours before portal closing, turning commercial compilation from a stressed midnight spreadsheet exercise into an audit-proof financial package:

  • Three-Way Grand Total Reconciliation: Verify that unit rates, extended subtotals, VAT (e.g., 15%), and final Grand Sum match to the exact decimal across the interactive portal BOQ form, the stamped PDF BOQ schedule, and the written words on the signed Financial Offer Letter—and confirm that your Initial Bank Guarantee covers 1%–2% of the final Grand Total inclusive of VAT.
  • Strict Technical Envelope Purification: Execute full-text searches inside every technical volume for currency symbols, OEM partner commercial quotes, staff monthly salaries, or license unit prices. Under public procurement law, even a forgotten cost footnote inside a technical appendix triggers immediate envelope disqualification.
  • BOQ-to-Scope Quantity Synchronization: Cross-check headcounts, license tiers, training batches, and maintenance years promised in the technical methodology against funded BOQ rows so technical evaluators and financial auditors see one unified, coherent bid.
Validating Alternative BOQ Structures & Provisional Sums: Inspect whether the BOQ includes Provisional Sums (مبالغ احتياطية), optional recurring extension years, or percentage-based local content preference formulas before running final margin calculations. Never alter, discount, or overwrite fixed Provisional Sum figures supplied by the procuring agency—carry them verbatim into your subtotal formula while confirming clearly whether VAT applies to optional line items under statutory tax guidance.

Finally, export the completed pricing schedule cleanly as a locked, digitally signed PDF matching official portal form figures to the exact decimal, storing your itemized underlying cost-buildup model internally with documented vendor quotes so finance teams can respond smoothly if an abnormally low price justification review is requested post-opening.

Arithmetic Verification & Envelope Firewall Audit: Prior to sealing portal envelopes, execute a joint Finance-QA arithmetic check confirming that horizontal quantity-times-unit-rate totals equal printed line totals down to the cent, VAT tax rows apply consistent statutory rates, written words match numeric grand totals identically, and zero unit prices or discount percentages were copied into any Technical Volume appendix table.
